# Watercolor art

Every painting on the business site is generated by `scripts/watercolor/` and committed as WebP in `public/img/paint`. Nothing is drawn by hand or stock: the same seed always paints the same picture. Photographs (the founder portrait) are shown as taken, never painted over.

```sh
pip install -r scripts/watercolor/requirements.txt
npm run generate:paintings              # everything, then the night versions
python3 scripts/watercolor/generate.py hero work-radio   # just some
python3 scripts/watercolor/night.py hero-fusion          # and their night versions
npm run generate:brand-images           # social cards reuse the paintings
```

## Art direction

"Wet ink, hard pixel": loose, transparent watercolor (the hand) meeting precise ink geometry and flat pixel grids (the code). The studio mark, two overlapping rings, is painted in cerulean and rose; where they overlap the glaze turns violet. Most paintings carry one digital element: a region resolved into palette-snapped pixels, or measured ink linework over the paint.

## How the renderer works (`engine.py`)

- **Washes** follow Tyler Hobbs' method: a polygon is deformed by recursive midpoint displacement, then dozens of further-deformed copies are stacked at low opacity, each masked by blotchy noise.
- **Pigment** is glazed with Beer–Lambert absorption in linear light, so overlapping colors mix like transparent paint.
- **Finishing** adds a wet-on-dry body, darker dried edges, low-frequency flow, blooms, and granulation from a periodic paper height field.
- **Pixel dissolve** snaps cells to the painting's own k-means palette and reveals them through an ordered-dither threshold, with optional breakaway cells.

| `app-wash-{1,2}{a,b}.webp`, `app-cta-{a,b}.webp` | App page hero and closing washes: alpha-only masks (encoded by `encode-mask.mjs`) tinted with each app's two pigments in CSS, so one set of paintings serves every app |
| `app-swatch.webp` | App page highlighter: a grey stroke on white, lightened over the app's pigment |

Paintings are rendered on white and shown with `mix-blend-mode: multiply`, so they glaze onto the page paper (at night, their versions in `night/` are screened). Don't put `transform`, `opacity`, `filter`, or `isolation` on an ancestor of a `.paint` image.

To add a case study, give it a painting function in `generate.py` (see `work_edd` for a compact example), register it in `ASSETS`, add it to `ASSETS` in `night.py`, and point the project's `art` at the new file.

## After dark (`night.py`)

The site follows the visitor's light or dark setting. On dark paper a multiplied painting would vanish, so every painting has a night version in `public/img/paint/night/`, derived from its daytime file:

- **Optical density.** Beer–Lambert, run backwards: each pixel's linear transmittance gives back how much paint is there, per channel. Its direction is the pigment (or the glaze of several); its length is the amount.
- **Pigment at an even lightness.** Each pigment is shown as it looks at one OKLab lightness (0.62), found by bisecting along its own absorption, so cerulean stays cerulean and rose stays rose. Coverage comes from the amount of paint, and dried edges and granulation glow a touch brighter, the way they read darker by day. Glazes that would sit near grey keep a floor of chroma, so the violet survives. Neutral ink linework turns to chalk.
- **Screened on black.** The result is painted on black and shown with `mix-blend-mode: screen`, so it glows on the night paper. `components/site/Paint.astro` serves the night file through `<picture>` when the visitor prefers dark.

Three kinds of art are handled differently. The closing call-to-action washes keep their daytime colour and texture as the same paint on dark paper would (lifted a little, edges feathered) so white type still reads on them. Highlighter swatches are RGBA and darker, because light type sits on them. The night paper is the daytime tooth (same seed) on a warm near-black sheet. App washes need no night files: they are masks, screened instead of multiplied, in pigments lightened by `src/data/app-pages/colours.ts`.
